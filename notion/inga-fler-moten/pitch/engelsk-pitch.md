# Engelsk pitch

**NOBODY NEEDS THIS MEETING**
*How knowledge workers got trapped in an industrial-era ritual — and how to get out*

---

Here's a number: **31 hours.** That's how many hours the average manager spends in meetings per week. Here's another number: **71%.** That's the percentage of senior executives who describe most of those meetings as unproductive. These are not new statistics. We've known this for years. And yet — nothing changes.

Every year, a new wave of books, frameworks, and LinkedIn manifestos promises to fix the meeting problem. Better agendas. Shorter time slots. "No meeting Fridays." Stand-ups. Async check-ins. Pre-reads. All of it assumes the meeting is the right format — we just need to run it better.

That assumption is wrong. And it's costing us more than we think.

The dirty secret hiding in plain sight: most modern meetings are not a feature of knowledge work — they're a hangover from two organizational cultures that have been obsolete for decades. The first is military command-and-control: the superior tells the subordinates what to do. The second is industrial administration: you account for inventory, report on status, hand off the baton. These cultures didn't survive in the sectors that invented them. The military moved on. The factory floor moved on. But somehow, the meeting — that great ceremony of hierarchical information transfer — survived, migrated into the open-plan office, multiplied on Zoom, and colonized our calendars.

Meanwhile, the actual nature of the work changed completely. Knowledge workers, as Peter Drucker pointed out thirty years ago, know their own work better than anyone above them in the org chart. They don't need to be told what to do. They don't need a status update they could read in thirty seconds on Slack. They don't need a presentation of the marketing plan they could open in Google Drive. What they need is time — protected, uninterrupted time — to actually think, create, build.

What the most effective individuals and organizations have figured out is not how to run better meetings. It's how to work *together* without meetings — and get dramatically more done as a result.

This book is the playbook for that shift. Not as an abstract philosophy. Not as a "framework." As a concrete, step-by-step account of what you do instead — how you restructure communication, decision-making, and collaboration so that the default is not a calendar invite, but work. Real work. The kind that produces something.

It is a book for knowledge workers who are tired of performing productivity. For managers who secretly know their weekly team meeting is theater. For organizations that want to stop mistaking activity for output.

And yes — there are some meetings you can't kill. This book will tell you which ones, and how to make them the exception rather than the rule.

**Genre:** Business / Organizational Culture / Productivity

**Comparable titles:** *Deep Work* (Cal Newport), *It Doesn't Have to Be Crazy at Work* (Fried & Heinemeier Hansson), *The Burnout Society* (Han) — but faster, more practical, more opinionated.

**Estimated length:** 55,000–70,000 words

**Format:** Narrative non-fiction with embedded methodology. Not a self-help manual. Not an airport paperback. A book people underline.
